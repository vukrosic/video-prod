# Mystery and "unexplained" video ideas

Mysteries give a viewer a reason to stay to the end: the question comes in the first 10 seconds and the
answer (or the honest "nobody knows") comes last. The strongest ones are **real, documented, still
open**, and **visual**. Every claim below needs a fact-check pass before scripting, as we did for episode 1.

## Unsolved science mysteries

### 1. "The 72 Seconds Nobody Can Explain" (the Wow! signal) ⭐
- **Hook:** In 1977 a radio telescope in Ohio caught a signal so strong the astronomer circled it and wrote
  "Wow!". It lasted 72 seconds and has never been heard again.
- **Why it works:** aliens-adjacent without being crackpot. The printout ("6EQUJ5") is an iconic image, and
  the explanations (hydrogen clouds, a flaring star, something else) make a natural countdown.
- **Visual:** the telescope's beam sweeping the sky; the signal rising and falling on a 72-second timer.

### 2. "Something Is Pulling Our Galaxy" (the Great Attractor)
- **Hook:** The Milky Way and thousands of galaxies around it are moving toward a point we can barely see,
  because our own galaxy is in the way.
- **Why it works:** scale plus threat plus "hidden behind the Milky Way". It's a great shader showcase.
- **Visual:** zoom out from Earth to the Laniakea supercluster, with flow lines converging.

### 3. "The Object That Didn't Behave Like a Rock" ('Oumuamua)
- **Hook:** In 2017 the first known interstellar object passed through our solar system, then sped up in a
  way ordinary gravity couldn't explain.
- **Why it works:** a real scientific debate (hydrogen ice? nitrogen fragment? a probe?) with credentialed
  people on each side.

### 4. "The Universe Is Expanding at Two Different Speeds" (the Hubble tension)
- **Hook:** Measure how fast the universe expands one way and you get one number. Measure it another way and
  you get a different one. Both are very precise.
- **Why it works:** a "physics might be broken" story, and very explainable with animation.

### 5. "The Fish Nobody Has Ever Seen Being Born" (European eel)
- **Hook:** People have eaten eels for thousands of years. No one has ever watched one spawn in the wild.
- **Why it works:** a small, relatable animal with a huge journey (to the Sargasso Sea). Aristotle thought
  they came from mud.

### 6. "The Human Species We Know From a Finger Bone" (Denisovans)
- **Hook:** Part of your DNA may come from a human species that we know mostly from one finger bone, a few
  teeth, and a jaw.
- **Why it works:** it's personal ("you carry them"), and it has detective-story structure.

### 7. "Lightning That Floats" (ball lightning)
- **Hook:** Thousands of people have reported glowing spheres that drift through rooms and pass through
  windows. Physics still has no agreed explanation.

### 8. "Why Do You Sleep?"
- **Hook:** You'll spend about a third of your life unconscious, and science still can't fully say why.
- **Why it works:** universal and relatable, with high click-through. It fits the "you" format of episode 1.

## Historical mysteries (solved or partly solved: "the answer is stranger than the myth")

### 9. "A 2,000-Year-Old Computer Found in a Shipwreck" (Antikythera mechanism)
- **Hook:** Divers found a lump of corroded bronze in 1901. X-rays showed dozens of gears that predicted
  eclipses.
- **Visual:** rebuild the gear train in 3D as it's explained.

### 10. "The Book No One Can Read" (Voynich manuscript)
- **Hook:** A 600-year-old book in an unknown script, full of plants that don't exist. Codebreakers,
  linguists and AI have all failed to read it.

### 11. "Nine Hikers, One Tent, Cut Open From the Inside" (Dyatlov Pass)
- **Hook:** the classic creepy setup, then a 2021 avalanche study that may finally explain it.
- **Why it works:** the "mystery solved" angle is fresher than yet another retelling of the legend.

## Series formats (reuse the episode 1 engine)

### 12. "How Long Would You Survive on Every Planet?"
The same survival timer, character and death counter, on new worlds. It's a direct sequel if episode 1
performs, and most of the shaders carry over.

### 13. "What's at the Bottom of Every Ocean Depth?"
A descent with a depth meter. Mystery comes built in, since most of the seafloor still isn't mapped in high
resolution.

### 14. "The Loudest Sound in History" (Krakatoa, 1883)
The sound was heard about 4,800 km away, and pressure gauges worldwide recorded the wave passing around the
planet several times. Short, shocking, and very visual.

## Recommendation

- **Next after episode 1:** #12, "Survive Every Planet". It reuses the engine, the character and the audience
  you'll have just built.
- **Best mystery bet:** #1, the Wow! signal. It has a tight 72-second hook, an iconic image and a real open
  question. #2 (Great Attractor) is the strongest visual showcase.
- **Safest broad-appeal pick:** #8, "Why Do You Sleep?"
